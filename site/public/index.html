the method, in 90 seconds

Most planners start with Monday. This one starts with the last page.

  1. 01

    Write the ending first.

    Before a single date, one page asks where you want the year to land. A paragraph, a sentence, a word. You write the ending the way a novelist does: before the rest exists, so the rest has somewhere to go.

  2. 02

    Break it into chapters.

    An ending is too far away to act on. Chapters are the three or four storylines that would have to be true for it to happen. They get names, not deadlines.

  3. 03

    Let it cascade.

    Each chapter leaves a trace on the month, the month on the week, the week on today. Nothing is copied twice; the links do the carrying. That’s the cascade, and it’s why the pages are hyperlinked instead of merely dated.

  4. 04

    Pick one goal.

    Every week and every day, one thing is circled. Circled means real. Everything else is weather.

  5. 05

    Write today’s plot.

    Today’s page is a scene, not a spreadsheet: the one goal, a short list, and a line about how it went. Done in your own hand, in about the time it takes a kettle to boil.

coffee first. legibility second.

today has excellent margins.

somewhere, a tuesday is proud of you.

ambition, but make it legible.

circled means real. everything else is weather.

dream in years, plan in weeks, forgive in days.

your future self reads this. wave.

rest is a task. check it off.

forty-eight lines like these live in the margins of the book.

what’s in the book

Dated for 2026–2027. Every page linked to every other.

  • i

    Cover

    Cream paper, copper rule, your name if you want it there. It opens like a book because it is one.

  • ii

    The method

    A few pages that explain the whole thing once, so the rest of the planner never has to nag.

  • iii

    Year & storylines

    The ending, written first. Then the chapters: three or four storylines with room to revise them as the year revises you.

  • iv

    Month

    A dated spread for every month with the month’s one goal up top and tabs that jump straight to any week.

  • v

    Week

    A dated spread for every week: its circled goal, seven generous columns, and a smile line in the margin.

  • vi

    Today

    A daily page for every date: one goal, a short list, a line for how it went. Handwriting counts as a font.

  • vii

    Archive & clearing

    Where finished chapters go to be remembered, and a clearing page for whatever didn’t make it. The archive remembers so you don’t have to.

Made for GoodNotes on iPad. It’s a hyperlinked PDF, so any notes app that follows links will do.
